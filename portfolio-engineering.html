Compact Ripple Tank

Design & CAD modeling

Problem. Design an instrument housing with a control panel, a wave-generating mechanism, and a lid that needed to move independently once imported into a real-time engine — meaning the CAD structure itself had to anticipate the downstream pipeline.

Approach. Modeled as a fully parametric assembly: base housing, control panel details, and mechanism built from driven sketches so dimensions could be revised without rebuilding geometry. Movable components (lid, panel switches) were kept as separate solid bodies and named clearly to survive the FBX export and stay addressable in Unreal.

Validation. Checked mass properties and interference between separated bodies before export; verified STEP and FBX exports preserved part hierarchy and pivot points needed for the animated lid.

Basic System & Photogates System

Design & CAD modeling

Problem. A physics lab track for kinematics and dynamics experiments — velocity/acceleration runs, collisions, and Atwood-machine-style pulley setups. In the physical lab, students reposition the photogates, end stops, and pulley along the track for each experiment; a VR training version needed that same freedom, not a single fused mesh of the whole rig.

Approach. Modeled as a 22-body assembly: the track itself as one continuous rail, with the cart, cart mass, pulley assembly, photogate, fence, rod, elastic bumper, and track end stop all kept as independent solid bodies constrained to slide along it. Each part was named to match its function (TrackMovablePart, TrackEndStop, PulleyWheel, PulleyBody, Photogate, Fence, Rod, CartMass, Car, ElasticBumper) so the intent survived the export, not just the geometry.

Validation. Confirmed the Maya outliner preserved every named part as a separate object after FBX import, then checked the Unreal scene to verify each movable component could be placed and repositioned independently along the track — the same reconfiguration a student would do at a physical bench.
